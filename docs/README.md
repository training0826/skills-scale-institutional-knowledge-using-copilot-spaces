# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

## Overview
OctoAcme uses a structured, lifecycle-based project management approach to deliver customer value in small, testable increments. Teams align on outcomes early, maintain clear ownership across roles, and use lightweight documentation to keep work discoverable, transparent, and easy to execute.

## Core Principles
- **Customer-first**: prioritize customer value and usability.
- **Iterative delivery**: deliver small, testable increments and adapt from feedback.
- **Clear ownership**: each project has accountable PM and Product leadership.
- **Data-informed decisions**: use metrics and evidence to guide trade-offs.
- **Psychological safety**: promote candid feedback, blameless learning, and collaboration.

## Project Lifecycle
1. **Initiation**: define the problem, stakeholders, success metrics, and high-level timeline.
2. **Planning**: break work into shippable increments with clear acceptance criteria.
3. **Execution**: build, test, review, and iterate with daily standups and regular demos.
4. **Release**: deploy to production with quality gates, verification, and rollback plans.
5. **Retrospective**: capture learnings, assign follow-up actions, and drive continuous improvement.

## Core Roles
- **Project Manager (PM)**: coordinates delivery plans, timelines, risks, dependencies, and stakeholder communication.
- **Product Manager (PdM)**: defines outcomes, prioritizes backlog, and measures business/customer impact.
- **Developers**: design, implement, test, and maintain features that meet acceptance criteria.
- **QA / Testing**: validates quality through test planning, execution, and release readiness checks.
- **Stakeholders**: provide domain input, priorities, approvals, and feedback on outcomes.

## Process Summary
OctoAcme runs projects through a consistent lifecycle of Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Retrospective. This model starts by validating business need and success metrics, then converts goals into a prioritized backlog and shippable milestones. The process is grounded in five core principles: customer-first delivery, iterative progress, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety.

Delivery is anchored by clear roles. Project Managers coordinate timelines, risks, dependencies, and stakeholder updates; Product Managers define desired outcomes and prioritization; and Developers own implementation quality and maintainability. QA/Testing contributes structured validation for acceptance criteria and release readiness, while stakeholders provide decision input and approvals at key gates.

Execution relies on a repeatable communication rhythm and workflow discipline. Teams use project boards to track progress across delivery states, daily or frequent standups to surface blockers, and sprint or milestone demos to keep stakeholders aligned. Pull request practices emphasize small, reviewable changes, CI validation, and explicit acceptance criteria, with escalation paths defined from team-level triage through sponsor-level decisions when risk increases.

Quality and improvement are treated as continuous responsibilities rather than end-of-project tasks. Unit, integration, smoke, and security checks are integrated into delivery and release processes, and each release includes verification and rollback planning. Retrospectives convert lessons learned into prioritized, owned action items, while ongoing risk register reviews and structured status communication keep execution transparent and resilient.
